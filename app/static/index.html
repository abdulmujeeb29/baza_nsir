                        ABOUT

                        Why Baza NISR

                        Baza means "ask" in Kinyarwanda. NISR publishes rich district statistics, but they are spread across separate portals and hundreds of Excel and PDF tables, mostly in English. NISR's own strategy (NSDS4, 2024 to 2029) lists the absence of an integrated data repository and too little use of Kinyarwanda as weaknesses, and calls for AI-assisted dissemination. Baza NISR is a working version of that layer.

                        How the agent works

                        1. It pre-selects likely tables from a catalogue of 35 cleaned NISR tables, then plans its steps.
                        2. It writes read-only SQL, runs independent queries in parallel, and joins surveys on the district key.
                        3. When the tables cannot answer, it searches NISR's own microdata catalogue and points to the right survey.
                        4. A verifier checks every number in the answer against the query results. Unmatched numbers are sent back once for correction, then shown as a warning.
                        5. Answers can be switched to Kinyarwanda at any time. A translation is shown only if it keeps exactly the same numbers.
                        6. Unanswered questions feed the data-gap radar for NISR.

                        Data

                        • 5th Population and Housing Census (RPHC5), 2022, main indicator tables.
                        • EICV7 2023/24 main indicators and the Utilities and Amenities thematic tables.
                        • Labour Force Survey quarterly tables up to Q2 2026.
                        • District boundaries: Open Data Rwanda (NISR) via geoBoundaries, CC BY 4.0.

                        Source: National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda, statistics.gov.rw, CC BY 4.0.

                        Limitations

                        • Census 2022 and EICV7 2023/24 differ in year and method, so comparisons across them are indicative.
                        • EICV7 district estimates come from a sample and have confidence intervals.
                        • Only loaded tables can be answered with numbers. Kinyarwanda answers are machine translated.

                        AI use disclosure

                        The problem framing, research, system design and product decisions are the team's: an agent over one cleaned database of NISR tables, verified numbers, NISR catalogue fallback, district briefs, Kinyarwanda with protected numbers and the data-gap radar. AI coding agents (including Claude Code) wrote much of the implementation from our design and instructions, under a plan with tests for every task, and the team reviewed the results. At runtime, a language model plans, writes SQL and phrases the answer, but never supplies the numbers: the verifier checks every one against NISR data.
